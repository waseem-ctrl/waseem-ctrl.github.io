Dumpster rental in Denver, CO: who publishes real numbers

A Denver contractor on Reddit said this spring that the city "seems to be in a race to the bottom on dumpster pricing." Good news for you: lots of Denver haulers now post their prices online. The catch is that a low sticker can hide a small weight allowance or a short rental window. Here's what six local operators actually publish, side by side.

How to read that table

  • Overage is almost always $75 a ton in Denver, so the real comparison is how many tons come with the bin. A 20-yard with 2 tons and one with 3 tons are a $75 difference before you've thrown anything away.
  • Dumpster Dogs' "no tonnage" option is a trap for most homeowners. $325 for a 20-yard looks cheapest, but with $75 a ton from the first pound, a 2-ton load costs $475 plus the fuel surcharge, which is more than the $450 package with 2 tons included. It only makes sense for very light junk.
  • Rental length splits the market. Premier, Centennial, and Red Rocks start at 7 days with $25 a day after. HBS and Dumpster Dogs include 14 days at $5 a day after. On a slow weekend remodel, that can be a $175 swing.
  • Heavy debris changes everything. Shingles, concrete, dirt, and tile hit 2 to 4 tons fast. Check our roofing weight guide and tell the dispatcher exactly what you're loading.

Driveway or street? The Denver permit question

On your own driveway or private property, the city's right-of-way rules don't apply (an HOA might still have its own). If the bin goes on the street, Denver's Department of Transportation and Infrastructure is strict:

  • Every dumpster or roll-off in the public right-of-way needs a Revocable Street Occupancy Permit, and only the dumpster company can get it. The company needs a Denver right-of-way dumpster license first (Denver ROW permits).
  • The city's own FAQ says the national companies "do not know Denver's permitting requirements" (DOTI FAQ). That's a strong hint to book a local operator if you need street placement.
  • The bin must sit in the parking lane, at least 20 feet from any corner, ramp, alley, or entrance, and can't block a sidewalk, tree lawn, travel lane, hydrant, or no-parking zone. The permit has to be visible on the container (one-time permit sheet).

What to do: ask the hauler, before you book, whether they hold a Denver right-of-way dumpster license, whether the street permit is included or extra, and how many days ahead they need. If they don't know what you're talking about, they're probably not local.

You might not need a dumpster at all

Denver Solid Waste Management customers get free Large Item Pickup every nine weeks on their regular trash day.

Large Item Pickup takesIt won't take
Up to 5 large items plus 10 bags (32 gallons, 50 lb max each): furniture, rugs, exercise equipment, non-electric bikes, large toys, bagged and wrapped mattresses, and bundled branches up to 4 ft long and 4 in thick. Out by 6 a.m. Construction materials (concrete, brick, fencing, pallets), appliances, electronics, auto parts, paint, batteries, tires, dirt, sod, stumps, and loose trash

The practical split: if your next pickup is close and you're clearing out a few pieces of furniture, wait for it. Anything with drywall, flooring, shingles, sod, or demo debris needs a dumpster. Setting items out on a non-pickup day counts as illegal dumping, so don't jump the gun.

Denver booking checklist

  1. Driveway or street? If street, do you hold a Denver right-of-way dumpster license, and is the permit included?
  2. How many tons are included, and is overage the usual $75/ton?
  3. How many days are included, and what's the daily rate after: $5 or $25?
  4. Any fuel surcharge or environmental fee on top of the quote?
  5. Any limits on concrete, dirt, sod, or shingles for the size I'm booking?
  6. What's the fee if the driver can't reach the bin (a blocked driveway, a parked car)?
  7. Will you send the landfill scale ticket with any overage bill?
