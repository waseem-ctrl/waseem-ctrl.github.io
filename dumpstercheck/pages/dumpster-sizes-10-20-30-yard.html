10 vs 20 vs 30 yard dumpster: which size you actually need

Yards measure volume. Landfills charge by weight. Pick for the densest stuff you’ll throw — not how empty the bin looks in the photo.

Quick pick

SizeBest forTypical weight riskSkip if…
10 yardGarage cleanout, small bath, light demoLow–mediumYou’re tearing off a roof or pouring concrete scraps
20 yardKitchen remodel, multi-room cleanout, small roofMedium–high on shingles/dirtNeighborhood bans larger bins and you only have light junk
30 yardWhole-home cleanout, large reno, new build scrapHigh if filled with dense debrisYou don’t need the volume — brokers love upselling these
40 yardCommercial / major constructionVery highMost residential streets (permits, HOA, parking)

10-yard roll-off

Compact
  • Cheapest base rate in most markets; fits tight driveways
  • Fine for furniture, boxes, light demo
  • Fills fast with bulky soft goods; don’t force dirt or concrete in

20-yard roll-off

Most common
  • Sweet spot for kitchen/bath renos and medium cleanouts
  • Roofing tear-offs: volume looks fine, weight blows past allowance — ask tons included before booking
  • Confirm driveway length; some 20s need more truck apron than a 10

30-yard roll-off

Volume play
  • Right when you’re emptying a house or stacking framing scrap
  • Easy to overbuy — if debris is dense, a 20 with honest tonnage often costs less than a “cheap” 30 with a 1-ton cap
  • Watch HOA / street-permit rules; bigger bins get ticketed more

Volume is not the bill

A half-full 30-yard of wet dirt can cost more than a full 10-yard of cardboard. Before you “go bigger to be safe,” get the included tons and $/ton overage in writing. See how dumpster rental pricing really works.

Three questions for the hauler

  1. For this size, how many tons are included?
  2. What’s prohibited (dirt, concrete, paint, tires) and the surcharge?
  3. Can I drop to a smaller size if the quote’s weight cap is tiny?
