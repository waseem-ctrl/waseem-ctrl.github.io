Dumpster rental in Austin, TX: who publishes real numbers

An Austin renter on Reddit put it well: pricing is "pretty consistent across all of the dumpster rental companies, so it really comes down to customer service." That's mostly true for the base price. Where Austin bills diverge is the fine print: included tons, the $/ton overage, extra days, and dry-run fees. Below are local operators that publish those numbers, so you can compare before you call.

How to read that table

  • Tons included matters more than sticker price. Grime Time and Dumpster Dogs both include 3 tons on a 20-yard. Captain Hook includes 2 tons on every size and bills actual tonnage above that, which is fine for light junk but adds up on remodels.
  • Overage rates are close ($60 to $85 a ton), so the bigger swing is how many tons you go over. A two-ton miss is roughly $120 to $170, not a disaster. The disaster is a heavy load in the wrong bin. See the roofing weight guide.
  • Extra days are cheap here. $5 to $10 a day at the two that publish it, so don't panic-fill a bin to beat the clock.
  • Heavy fill has its own rules. Grime Time allows full concrete/gravel/soil loads only in a 10-yard and half loads of concrete in a 20-yard. Captain Hook says dirt, brick, concrete, and gravel can't go in its 20- or 30-yard bins. Tell the dispatcher what you're loading.

Driveway or street? The Austin permit question

On your own driveway or private lot, you generally don't need a city permit (your HOA may still have rules). In the street, curb, alley, or sidewalk, it's different:

  • Austin's code bars placing bulky items, including containers used for work or waste, on a street, alley, or sidewalk unless authorized (City Code ch. 14-9).
  • A dumpster in the public right-of-way needs a Right-of-Way permit, and the city lists insurance and a $10,000 bond as requirements for dumpster permits (Austin Transportation and Public Works). That's why most homeowners can't practically pull this permit themselves.
  • Related right-of-way requests need at least 3 business days of notice (ROW permits page).

What to do: if the bin can't go on your driveway, ask the hauler whether they hold a right-of-way license and will pull the permit, what it adds to the price, and how many days it takes. If they shrug, keep calling.

Austin booking checklist

  1. Driveway or street? If street, who pulls the right-of-way permit and what does it cost?
  2. How many tons are included, and what's the $/ton after that?
  3. How many days are included, and what's the daily rate after?
  4. Any heavy-fill limits on the size you're booking (concrete, dirt, brick, gravel)?
  5. What's the dry-run fee, and what counts as "blocked" (a parked car, a locked gate)?
  6. Is there a deposit, and is it refundable?
  7. Will they send the landfill scale ticket with any overage bill?
