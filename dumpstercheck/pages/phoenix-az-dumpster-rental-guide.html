Dumpster rental in Phoenix, AZ: the city bin vs local haulers

Phoenix has something most cities don't: the city itself rents roll-off dumpsters to residents. In an r/phoenix thread this August, most people who'd used it said they'd do it again. It isn't the right call for everyone, though. Here's the city's deal next to five local haulers that publish their terms, with the math worked out.

Option 1: rent from the City of Phoenix

From the city's dumpster rental page, checked 2026-10-31:

  • $165 per pull (delivery, pickup, and emptying) plus $55 per ton of whatever the bin weighs at pickup. No tons are included; you pay for every one, but at a low rate.
  • Sizes: 10, 20, 30, and 40 yard. A customer service specialist decides the size with you after you submit the request form, and Public Works says it will contact you within 48 hours.
  • Up to 10 days, with an extra fee beyond that. You need an active city services account with no past-due balance, and you sign a liability release and service contract. No hazardous waste.

The trade-offs locals mention: nothing is confirmed until the city calls you back, so it's slower to book than a company with an online checkout. One remodeler who used three city bins said heavy material (concrete, brick, dirt) was limited to the smaller size. And one contractor in a 2024 thread said the city was a pain compared with private haulers, so experiences vary.

  • If you have a city services account and can wait a couple of days, the city wins at every weight here, because $55 a ton is well under the $72 to $80 private haulers charge, and the base fee is low.
  • Among private haulers, it depends on weight. Devil Dog's published rates are the lowest at every weight in this table, and Curbside's 3 included tons keep it just $10 behind Devil Dog on any load of 3 tons or more.
  • Rental days matter more than you'd think. Rapid charges $50 a day after 7 days and redbox+ charges $20. A two-week remodel at Rapid adds $350 in day fees, while redbox+'s 14-day package is only $50 more than its 7-day.
  • Tell the dispatcher what you're loading. Concrete, rock, dirt, and tile are heavy fast, and both the city and private haulers may limit bin size for them. Our roofing weight guide shows how quickly shingles add up.

Driveway or street? The Phoenix permit question

A bin on your own driveway generally doesn't need a city permit, though your HOA may have rules. Putting it in a public street or right-of-way is different: the City of Phoenix Street Transportation Department regulates this, and it publishes a document called "Storage of Material and Roll-Off Bins in the Right-of-Way" on its street project resources page. Temporary lane restrictions go through the department's Right-of-Way Management office.

What to do: if the bin can't go on your property, ask the hauler before booking whether they handle the Phoenix right-of-way paperwork, whether it costs extra, and how much lead time they need. In the suburbs (Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Scottsdale, Tempe), the rules belong to that city, not Phoenix.

You might not need a dumpster at all

Phoenix moved bulk trash to appointments only. Each household gets four pickups a year, booked at least 3 business days ahead online or at 602-262-6251.

Bulk trash takesIt won't take
One pile up to 10 cubic yards (about 17 ft × 4 ft × 4 ft) within 5 feet of the curb: furniture, small appliances without refrigerant, toilets, bagged yard waste, and tree trimmings under 12 inches thick and 4 feet long. Out by 5:30 a.m., and no more than 7 days early. Bricks, concrete, dirt, rock, asphalt, roofing shingles or tiles; construction debris over 25 lb or 4 ft long; tires; glass doors, windows, and mirrors; refrigerators, freezers, and AC units; hazardous waste; loose grass and leaves; more than 2 pallets

The practical split: a furniture clear-out fits bulk trash for free. Any real demo or roofing debris needs a dumpster. The city has also said residents can take up to one ton a month to a city transfer station for free with an ID and a city services bill (city announcement), which works if you have a truck or trailer. The scheduler also blocks out the week after Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas, and New Year's, so plan around those dates.

Phoenix booking checklist

  1. Do I have a current City of Phoenix services account? If yes, get the city's quote first.
  2. How many tons are included, and is overage $72, $75, or $80 a ton?
  3. How many days are included, and what's the daily rate after: $20, $35, or $50?
  4. Are delivery and pickup included, or charged separately?
  5. Any size limit for concrete, rock, dirt, or tile?
  6. What are the failed-delivery, overfill, and cancellation fees?
  7. If the bin goes on the street, who handles the right-of-way permit?
  8. Will you send the landfill scale ticket with any overage bill?
